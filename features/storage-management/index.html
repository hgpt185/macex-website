Free up storage on your Mac without deleting the wrong thing

Updated November 15, 2026

Short answer: Open Free Up Space in MacEx, or press ⇧⌘K. It sorts what it finds into Safe to clear (old installers, forgotten downloads, old screenshots, empty files) and Your call (large files, big old videos, duplicates, similar photos, unused apps). Nothing is ticked for you. You open each group, pick files, review the list, and only then do they move to Trash, where you can still restore them.

Start with what macOS already shows

macOS has its own overview in System Settings › General › Storage (Apple’s guide to freeing up storage). It breaks usage into categories such as Applications, Documents and Photos, and offers recommendations like storing files in iCloud or emptying Trash automatically. If one category is clearly the problem, such as a Photos library or old iPhone backups, deal with it there first.

Where that view gets harder is the everyday clutter spread across folders: installers you ran once, downloads you never opened again, screen recordings and exported videos, and copies of the same file in three places. That is what MacEx Free Up Space is for.

What Free Up Space looks for

Each group has a plain rule, so you can tell why a file is listed. “Old” is 90 days unless you change it with the Older than menu at the top right (30, 60, 90, 180 or 365 days).

How cleanup stays reversible

  1. Click a row to see every file inside it.
  2. Tick what you want gone. Nothing is ticked for you, and Select all safe only selects the Safe to clear groups.
  3. Review the list, then move it to Trash.

MacEx never deletes a file for good. Files sit in the normal Trash until you empty it, and MacEx keeps a history of the last 500 files it moved there, with their original folders. Click the Trash icon in the MacEx sidebar to put any of them back. Restoring never replaces a file that is already there. Once you empty the Trash, those files can’t be restored.

Why the space you get back can differ

  • Trash still counts. Space comes back when you empty the Trash, not when files move into it.
  • APFS shares data. Copies made in Finder can be clones that share storage, and Time Machine local snapshots can hold on to deleted files for a while. Removing a clone may free less than its listed size.
  • Compression and hard links also make a file’s listed size differ from the disk space it uses.

What it doesn’t touch

  • System and app data. MacEx works with your own files and apps. It doesn’t clear caches, logs or anything inside ~/Library, and hidden files are left out on purpose.
  • Online-only iCloud files. They are never downloaded, so they’re never hashed or offered for cleanup.
  • Folders macOS hasn’t allowed. MacEx runs as you and can’t read a folder you haven’t given it access to.

Keep it tidy automatically (optional)

Automation rules are off until you turn them on in Settings › Automation, and they only work in folders you added yourself with Add Folder. One rule moves .dmg and .pkg files to Trash after the number of days you set; another files screenshots into a folder for each month. Rules check every 30 minutes while MacEx is in the foreground and never delete a file for good.
