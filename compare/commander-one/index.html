MacEx vs Commander One: which should you use?

Updated November 20, 2026. Commander One facts checked on Eltima’s site and the Mac App Store that day.

Short answer: Commander One is a classic two-panel file manager in the style of Total Commander. The free version has unlimited tabs, custom hotkeys and regex search; the PRO pack ($29.99 on the Mac App Store) adds FTP, SFTP, cloud drives, a terminal and phone mounting. MacEx is built around finding and cleaning up files: describe a file to find it, search the words inside screenshots, see where downloads came from, and free up space with a review step, plus two panes and batch rename, for $9.99 once.

Where Commander One is the better choice

Free to start. Two panels, unlimited tabs, custom hotkeys, regex and content search, a hex viewer and root access cost nothing.

Servers, clouds and devices (PRO). FTP, SFTP, FTPS, WebDAV, Dropbox, Google Drive, OneDrive, Amazon S3 and others, plus mounting iPhone, Android and MTP devices and a terminal emulator. MacEx has SFTP and SMB only.

Total Commander habits. If you came from Total Commander on Windows, Commander One will feel familiar; Eltima says Total Commander’s author approved it as “Total Commander for Mac”. See Total Commander for Mac: the alternatives.

Where MacEx is the better choice

Finding files without knowing names or regex. MacEx gathers your folders into one library by kind and reads plain sentences such as pdfs from last week over 5 mb. Text Search finds words inside screenshots and scanned PDFs. How MacEx search works.

Cleanup with a safety net. Free Up Space and the duplicate finder show you every file first, move only what you select to the normal Trash, and remember the last 500 files so you can put them back. What Free Up Space checks.

A Mac-style app. MacEx looks and behaves like a Mac app: a sidebar, previews, Finder tags and Shortcuts actions. Commander One’s panels look like a classic Windows commander.

Price once you need servers. MacEx is $9.99 with SFTP and SMB included. Commander One’s server features need PRO at $29.99.

Switching from Commander One, or using both

MacEx needs no import: it reads your folders and Finder tags in place. Open Split View with ⌘\; F5 copies and F6 moves to the other pane, the keys Total Commander made familiar. Use ⇧⌘G to go to a folder by path.

Keep Commander One’s free version for tabs and regex, or PRO for clouds and phones, and use MacEx to find files and clear space. They don’t interfere with each other.

Verdict: MacEx or Commander One?

  • Choose Commander One if you want a free two-panel manager with tabs and custom hotkeys, or need FTP, cloud drives and phone mounting (PRO).
  • Choose MacEx if you lose track of files and run low on space, want a Mac-style app, and want two panes, batch rename and SFTP for $9.99 once.
  • Use both if you like Commander One’s panels and want MacEx’s search and cleanup.

Frequently asked questions

Is Commander One free?

The basic version is free: two panels, unlimited tabs, custom hotkeys, search and zip. FTP, SFTP, cloud drives, device mounting, a terminal and themes need the PRO pack, which is $29.99 on the Mac App Store.

Is MacEx a good Commander One alternative?

Yes, if you want help finding and cleaning up files as well as two panes. MacEx adds search by description, text search in images, download sources and reviewed cleanup. It lacks Commander One’s tabs, custom hotkeys, FTP and cloud drives.

Does MacEx support FTP or Google Drive?

No. MacEx connects to SFTP servers and SMB shares only. Commander One PRO and ForkLift support FTP and cloud drives.

Does MacEx use F5 and F6 to copy and move?

Yes. In Split View, F5 copies the selection to the other pane and F6 moves it, the keys Total Commander made familiar.

Which looks more like a Mac app?

MacEx. It uses a Mac sidebar, previews and Finder tags. Commander One follows the classic two-panel commander look.

Is there a free alternative to Commander One?

Marta, Nimble Commander and Double Commander are free two-pane file managers for Mac. MacEx costs $9.99 once. Eight Finder alternatives compared.
