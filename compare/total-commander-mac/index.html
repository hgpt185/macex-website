Total Commander for Mac: the alternatives

Updated November 20, 2026. Facts checked on each developer’s site that day.

Short answer: There is no Total Commander for Mac. Ghisler Software makes it for Windows only. On a Mac, Double Commander (free, open source) is the closest copy and even runs Total Commander plugins. Commander One is a polished free alternative with a paid PRO pack, and Nimble Commander is free and very fast. MacEx keeps the two panes and F5/F6 copy and move, and adds what no commander offers: describe a file to find it, and free up space with a review step, for $9.99 once.

Where a Total Commander clone is the better choice

Muscle memory. If you used Total Commander for years, Double Commander gives you the same layout, F-key bar, plugins, viewer (F3) and editor (F4). It is free and has builds for Apple silicon and Intel Macs.

Tabs, archives, FTP and plugins. All three commanders have tabs. Double Commander and Nimble Commander browse archives like folders and connect to FTP and SFTP. MacEx has none of tabs, plugins or FTP.

Price. Double Commander, Nimble Commander and Commander One’s basic version are free.

Where MacEx is the better choice

It feels like a Mac app. A sidebar, previews, Finder tags and Shortcuts actions, with the two panes and F5/F6 you’re used to. File management in MacEx.

Finding files without browsing. No commander does this: MacEx gathers your folders into one library and reads sentences like zips bigger than 1gb older than 6 months. It also finds words inside screenshots and scanned PDFs. How it works.

Freeing up space safely. Free Up Space lists old installers, forgotten downloads and big old videos, the duplicate finder checks byte for byte, and everything goes to the normal Trash with a 500-file history. What it checks.

Moving from Total Commander to a Mac

  • Copy and move: in MacEx Split View, F5 copies and F6 moves to the other pane. On a MacBook, hold fn with the F key, or turn on “Use F1, F2, etc. keys as standard function keys” in System Settings › Keyboard › Keyboard Shortcuts › Function Keys.
  • Rename: in Finder press Return; in MacEx select files and choose batch rename.
  • Delete: ⌘⌫ moves to the Trash. MacEx never deletes for good.
  • Go to a path: ⇧⌘G in both Finder and MacEx.
  • Hidden files: ⇧⌘. in Finder; a switch in MacEx.

If you only need Total Commander itself, you can run the Windows version in a Windows virtual machine on your Mac. For most people a native Mac app is simpler. Switching from Windows in general? See File Explorer for Mac.

Verdict: what to use instead of Total Commander on a Mac

  • Choose Double Commander if you want Total Commander as close as possible, plugins included, for free.
  • Choose Commander One if you want a free, more Mac-polished two-panel app, with PRO for FTP and cloud drives.
  • Choose MacEx if you want two panes and F5/F6 in a Mac-style app that also finds files from a description and frees up space safely, for $9.99 once.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a Total Commander for Mac?

No. Ghisler Software makes Total Commander for Windows only. On a Mac, use a commander-style alternative such as Double Commander, Commander One or Nimble Commander, or a two-pane app such as MacEx.

What is the closest Total Commander alternative for Mac?

Double Commander. It is free and open source, was inspired by Total Commander, keeps its layout and F-key bar, and supports Total Commander WCX, WDX, WFX and WLX plugins.

Is Commander One the same as Total Commander?

No. Commander One is made by Electronic Team (Eltima), not Ghisler Software. Eltima says Total Commander’s author approved it as “Total Commander for Mac”. The basic version is free; servers and cloud drives need PRO.

Can I run Total Commander on a Mac?

Only the Windows version, inside a Windows virtual machine. There is no native macOS version.

Does MacEx use F5 and F6 like Total Commander?

Yes. In Split View, F5 copies the selection to the other pane and F6 moves it. MacEx doesn’t copy the rest of Total Commander’s layout: it has no tabs, plugins or built-in editor.

Is there a free Total Commander alternative for Mac?

Yes: Double Commander and Nimble Commander are free and open source, and Commander One has a free version. MacEx costs $9.99 once.
