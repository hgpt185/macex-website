MacEx vs Bloom: which Finder replacement?

Updated November 20, 2026. Bloom facts checked on bloomapp.club that day.

Short answer: Bloom is a refined Finder look-alike: dual and quad-pane layouts, column view, a system-wide Go to Folder panel, workspaces, colour themes, iCloud Drive, Dropbox and OneDrive, and undo for every operation, for $16 once with a 7-day trial. MacEx costs $9.99 once and is built around finding and clearing out files: describe a file to find it, search the words inside screenshots, see where every download came from, and free up space with a review step, plus two panes, batch rename, SFTP and SMB. Pick Bloom for layouts and looks, MacEx for search and cleanup.

Where Bloom is the better choice

Layouts and looks. Bloom has dual and quad-pane layouts, column view with automatic resizing, a compact Portal window you can pin on top, sync browsing and a colour theme you pick. MacEx has one or two panes in grid or list view.

Getting around fast. Bloom’s Go to Folder panel works from any app, and almost every shortcut can be changed. In MacEx only the Quick Search shortcut can be changed.

Extras. Undo for every operation, image conversion, new-file templates, a disk treemap and one-click Terminal. And a 7-day trial; MacEx has no trial.

Where MacEx is the better choice

Finding a file you can’t place. MacEx gathers your folders into one library sorted by kind and reads plain sentences such as pdfs from gmail last month or zips bigger than 1gb older than 6 months. Text Search finds the words inside screenshots and scanned PDFs. How MacEx search works.

Clearing space safely. Bloom’s treemap shows where the space went; MacEx also lists old installers, forgotten downloads, big old videos, unused apps and exact duplicates, and you review each before it goes to the normal Trash. What Free Up Space checks.

Servers. SFTP servers and SMB shares open in the same window as your files. SFTP and SMB in MacEx.

Price. $9.99 once, against $16.

Switching from Bloom, or using both

MacEx reads your folders and Finder tags where they are; nothing to import. Add the folders you work in to the sidebar, press ⌘\ for Split View, and F5 or F6 to copy or move to the other pane.

Both apps work with the same folders and tags, so you can browse in Bloom and use MacEx to find files and free up space.

Verdict: MacEx or Bloom?

  • Choose Bloom if you want a nicer-looking Finder with quad panes, column view, themes, cloud drives and undo for everything, and like trying before you buy.
  • Choose MacEx if you lose track of files or run low on space, want SFTP and SMB, and want to pay less: $9.99 once.
  • Use both if you love Bloom’s layouts and want MacEx’s search and cleanup.

Frequently asked questions

Is MacEx a good Bloom alternative?

Yes, if you mainly want to find files and free up space. MacEx has two panes, batch rename, SFTP and SMB, and adds search by description, text search in images and reviewed cleanup. It has no quad panes, column view, themes or Terminal button.

How much does Bloom cost?

Bloom costs $16 once, and its site says that includes all future updates with no subscription. It has a 7-day free trial.

Does Bloom have a free trial?

Yes, 7 days. MacEx has no free trial, but you can try a live demo with sample files in your browser.

Can MacEx show four folders at once like Bloom?

No. MacEx Split View shows two folders side by side.

Which is better for freeing up disk space?

MacEx. Bloom’s Folder Scan shows a treemap of what fills the disk; MacEx also lists what is usually safe to clear, checks duplicates byte for byte, and moves only what you pick to the Trash.

Do Bloom and MacEx need the same macOS version?

Yes. Both need macOS 14 Sonoma or later.
