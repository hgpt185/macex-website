MacEx vs QSpace: which Finder alternative?

Updated November 20, 2026. QSpace facts checked on qspace.awehunt.com and the Mac App Store that day.

Short answer: QSpace looks like Finder and adds 12 window layouts, an address bar you can type in, hashes, Windows key bindings and deep customization. Server connections, advanced batch rename, the Stash Shelf and the enhanced archiver are paid extensions on top of the app. MacEx includes everything for $9.99 once: describe a file to find it, search the words inside screenshots, see where downloads came from, free up space with a review step, two panes, batch rename, SFTP and SMB.

Where QSpace is the better choice

Layouts. QSpace switches between 12 window layouts for comparing, copying and moving files between folders. MacEx has one or two panes.

Customization and Windows habits. An address bar you type into, Windows key bindings, custom file templates, hashes, and one-click Terminal or editor for the current folder.

Servers and archives, if you buy the extensions. Over 20 server and cloud types and over 20 archive formats. MacEx has SFTP, SMB and zip only.

A Pro trial. QSpace Pro can be tried for 30 days. MacEx has a browser demo but no trial.

Where MacEx is the better choice

One price, everything included. MacEx is $9.99 with SFTP, SMB, batch rename and workspaces built in. QSpace’s equivalents are extensions on top of the app.

Finding files. MacEx sorts your folders into one library by kind and reads sentences such as pdfs from gmail last month. Text Search finds the words in screenshots and scans. How MacEx search works.

Cleanup. Free Up Space and the byte-for-byte duplicate finder show you what is safe to clear, move only what you pick to the Trash, and can put back any of the last 500 files. What Free Up Space checks.

Switching from QSpace, or using both

MacEx reads your folders and Finder tags where they are; there is nothing to import. Add the folders you use to the sidebar, open Split View with ⌘\, and press F5 to copy or F6 to move to the other pane. A workspace does the job of a shelf you want to keep.

If QSpace’s layouts are what you love, keep them for browsing and use MacEx to find files and free up space.

Verdict: MacEx or QSpace?

  • Choose QSpace if you want a Finder look-alike with many layouts, Windows key bindings and deep customization, and will buy the extensions you need.
  • Choose MacEx if you want one price with everything included, plus search by description, text in images and safe cleanup that QSpace doesn’t offer.
  • Use both if you browse in QSpace and want MacEx to find and clear out files.

Frequently asked questions

How much does QSpace cost?

QSpace Pro lists at $29.99 and the Mac App Store edition at $27.99, both often discounted. Server Connections ($9.99), Advanced Batch Rename, Stash Shelf and Enhanced Archiver ($4.99 each, list) are sold separately.

Is MacEx a good QSpace alternative?

Yes, if you want everything in one price and care about finding and cleaning up files. MacEx has fewer layouts (one or two panes) and no Windows key bindings.

What is the difference between QSpace and QSpace Pro?

QSpace is sold on the Mac App Store; QSpace Pro is sold on the developer’s site with a 30-day trial. The developer’s pricing page lists them separately and explains the differences.

Can MacEx show more than two folders at once?

No. MacEx Split View shows two folders side by side. QSpace offers 12 layouts if you want more.

Does MacEx need extensions for SFTP or batch rename?

No. SFTP, SMB, batch rename, zip and workspaces are all included in the $9.99 price.

Does MacEx work on macOS Catalina or Big Sur?

No. MacEx needs macOS 14 Sonoma or later. QSpace supports older systems, from macOS 10.15.
