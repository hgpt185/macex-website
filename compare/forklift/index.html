MacEx vs ForkLift: which Mac file manager fits you?

Updated November 20, 2026. ForkLift facts checked on binarynights.com that day.

Short answer: ForkLift 4 is the stronger app for moving files between servers: tabs, folder sync and a dozen remote protocols. MacEx is the stronger app for finding and clearing out files on your Mac: describe a file to find it, search the words inside screenshots, see where every download came from, and free up space with a review step. Both have two panes and batch rename. MacEx costs $9.99 once with updates included; ForkLift costs $19.95 with one year of updates.

Where ForkLift is the better choice

Servers and cloud storage. ForkLift connects to SFTP, FTP, WebDAV, Amazon S3, Backblaze B2, Google Drive, OneDrive, Dropbox, SMB, AFP and NFS, can copy between two servers by drag and drop, and queues transfers with bandwidth limits. MacEx covers SFTP and SMB only, and its SFTP doesn’t copy whole folders or delete on the server.

Folder sync. ForkLift compares two folders and syncs them one or two ways, and can save a sync as a favourite. MacEx has no sync.

Tabs and remote editing. ForkLift has tabs in each pane and uploads a remote file again when you save it in your editor. MacEx has neither.

If most of your day is moving files to and from servers, ForkLift is built for that and MacEx isn’t.

Where MacEx is the better choice

Finding a file you can’t place. MacEx gathers your folders into one library sorted by kind. Type what you remember, such as zips bigger than 1gb older than 6 months or pdfs from gmail last month, and it turns the sentence into filters you can see and edit. How MacEx search works.

Words inside images. Turn on Text Search and MacEx reads the text in screenshots and scanned PDFs on your Mac, so screenshots with wifi password finds the photo of a router label.

Freeing up space without fear. Free Up Space lists old installers, forgotten downloads, old screenshots, big old videos, unused apps and exact duplicates. You review every file, only what you select goes to the Trash, and MacEx can put back any of the last 500 files it moved. What Free Up Space checks.

Price. $9.99 once with updates included, against $19.95 for a year of ForkLift updates.

What each costs over three years

Both are paid once, but ForkLift’s licence includes a fixed period of updates. After it ends, you keep the last version your licence covers, or buy a new licence to keep updating. Single-user list prices, checked on the date above:

Switching from ForkLift, or using both

There is nothing to import. MacEx reads your existing folders and Finder tags and leaves files where they are. Add the folders you work in to the sidebar, and press ⌘\ for Split View. F5 copies and F6 moves to the other pane, the keys commander-style file managers such as Total Commander made familiar.

Many people will want both. Keep ForkLift for server work and sync, and use MacEx to find files, sort downloads and clear space. They don’t conflict: neither moves your files unless you ask.

Verdict: MacEx or ForkLift?

  • Choose ForkLift if you transfer files to servers or cloud storage every day, sync folders, or need tabs.
  • Choose MacEx if you lose files, fight with your Downloads folder or keep running out of space, and want two panes and batch rename for everyday work.
  • Choose MacEx if price matters: $9.99 once, updates included, no subscription.
  • Use both if you do heavy server work and also want to find and clean up files on your Mac.

Frequently asked questions

Is MacEx a good ForkLift alternative?

Yes, if you mainly work with files on your own Mac. MacEx has two panes, batch rename, tags, SMB and SFTP, and adds search by description, text search in images and reviewed cleanup. It isn’t a full replacement for ForkLift’s server features: it has no FTP, WebDAV, cloud drives or folder sync.

Is ForkLift 4 a one-time purchase?

Yes. Binarynights sells a lifetime licence that includes one or two years of updates ($19.95 or $34.95 for one user). After that you can keep using the last version your licence covers, or buy a new licence for newer versions.

Does MacEx have tabs like ForkLift?

No. MacEx shows one location in each pane of Split View. If you rely on tabs, ForkLift or Finder suits that better.

Can MacEx sync folders like ForkLift?

No. MacEx doesn’t compare or sync folders. ForkLift does, locally and on servers.

Which is better for SFTP, MacEx or ForkLift?

ForkLift. It is a complete transfer client with queues, remote editing and many protocols. MacEx can browse an SFTP server, upload, download, rename and create folders after checking the host key, but it doesn’t transfer whole folders or delete on the server.

Can I use MacEx and ForkLift on the same Mac?

Yes. Both read your normal folders and Finder tags, and neither reorganizes your files unless you ask. Many people use ForkLift for transfers and MacEx for finding and cleaning up.

Is there a free alternative to ForkLift?

Commander One has a free version, and Marta and Nimble Commander are free. All three are two-pane file managers. MacEx isn’t free, but it costs $9.99 once. Eight Finder alternatives compared.
